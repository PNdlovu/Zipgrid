## 1. Market Context — Why Now

### The UK EV Transition
The United Kingdom is one of the most accelerated EV adoption markets in Europe, driven by a combination of government policy, consumer demand, and infrastructure investment:

- **2035 ban** on the sale of new petrol and diesel cars (confirmed UK government policy)
- **ULEZ expansion** across London and other cities penalising ICE vehicles in urban centres
- **Workplace Charging Scheme (WCS)** and **Electric Vehicle Homecharge Scheme (EVHS)** — government grants reducing home charger installation cost to £350 or below
- **Smart Export Guarantee (SEG)** — financial incentive for EV + solar households
- Rising fuel costs making EV total cost of ownership compelling for both households and businesses

### The Gap
Home chargers are installed at scale but sit idle for 18–22 hours per day. The public network is growing but remains undersupplied relative to the EV fleet — and completely unsuitable for guaranteed, affordable, local charging. **The private charging asset base is massively underutilised and unmonetised.**

## 3. Market Segments Deep Dive

### 3.1 Homeowners
**Size:** ~700,000 UK homes with EV charger installed (growing ~150,000/year)

**Pain points:**
- Charger sits idle 18–22 hrs/day — no way to monetise it
- Electricity tariffs are complex — hard to manually optimise charging times
- Faults require navigating to separate brand apps (Ohme, Zappi, EO) — no unified view
- No voice control — all management is app-tap-based

**Willingness to pay:** £5–£10/month for automation and passive income potential

**Acquisition trigger:** New charger installation (installer channel), energy tariff switch (utility channel), social recommendation

### 3.2 Small Businesses
**Size:** ~50,000 UK SMBs with at least one EV charge point (growing fast)

**Sub-segments:**
- Hospitality (cafés, restaurants, pubs): ~15,000
- Retail (shops, supermarkets): ~10,000
- Fitness & leisure (gyms, sports centres): ~5,000
- Landlords (residential and commercial): ~12,000
- Other services (salons, clinics, offices): ~8,000

**Pain points:**
- No simple revenue dashboard — earnings are opaque
- No dynamic pricing tools — charging at flat rate 24/7 regardless of demand
- Customer access control is manual (key fobs, phone calls)
- No integration between charger and till/POS system

**Willingness to pay:** £25–£100/month depending on charger count and feature tier

### 3.3 Installers & Service Technicians
**Size:** ~4,000 OZEV-certified installers in the UK; ~3,000 independent EV maintenance firms

**Pain points:**
- Lead generation is expensive and inconsistent
- No centralised platform to receive verified, local job leads
- Reputation is hard to build without a trusted review system
- Quoting and scheduling is manual

**Willingness to pay:** 10–15% commission on booked jobs or £50–£100/month listing fee

### 3.4 EV Drivers (P2P)
**Size:** ~1.1M EV owners; ~200,000 actively seeking affordable, local charging alternatives

**Pain points:**
- Public charger dead zones in suburbs and rural areas
- No guaranteed charger availability — public chargers are first-come-first-served
- Public fast-charging is expensive (£0.65–£0.85/kWh at bp pulse, Pod Point rapids)
- Overnight charging at hotels/Airbnbs often not available

## 4. Market Trends & Tailwinds

| Trend | Impact on Zipgrid |
|-------|------------------|
| **2035 ICE ban** | Accelerates EV fleet growth → more potential hosts and drivers every year |
| **Smart Meter rollout** | 80%+ UK homes with smart meters by 2026 → easier tariff integration |
| **Octopus Agile / Go tariffs** | Time-of-use tariffs create strong incentive for smart charging scheduling |
| **ULEZ expansion** | Urban EV conversion accelerates → denser urban charger supply possible |
| **OZEV grants** | Reduces homeowner charger install cost → more hosts available |
| **AI consumer normalisation** | Voice assistants in 60%+ UK homes → low friction for voice-first product |
| **Energy price sensitivity** | Post-2022 energy crisis → UK consumers highly motivated to cut electricity costs |
| **Property EV readiness requirements** | New UK builds required to include EV charge points from 2022 → growing supply base |

## 5. Market Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Incumbent response** — Pod Point / BP Pulse add marketplace | Medium | Move fast; lock in installer and host network before they react |
| **Regulatory shift** — government changes EV grant rules | Low–Medium | Maintain government affairs awareness; diversify revenue beyond grants |
| **Hardware fragmentation** — OCPP adoption slower than expected | Medium | Support non-smart chargers via manual session confirmation (fallback) |
| **EV adoption slowdown** — consumer hesitancy | Low | Market trend too strong to reverse; government policy committed |
| **Energy supplier pivot** — Octopus / EDF build own P2P platform | Low | First-mover and network effects protect position; seek exclusive bundles |
| **Trust failure** — safety incident damages brand | Low–Medium | Robust insurance, incident protocol, and rapid response team |
