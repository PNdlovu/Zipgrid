## 2. Competitor Deep Dives (Updated)

### 2.1 Zap-Map

**What they are:** The UK's dominant public charger discovery and community app. 500,000+ users, 30,000+ mapped charge points, community-reported availability.

**Pricing:**
- Free: map, live data, Zap-Pay, max 3 saved routes, ads
- Premium £29.99/yr (£2.50/mo) or £3.49/mo: unlimited routes, enhanced filters, CarPlay/Android Auto, ad-free, 5% Zap-Pay discount, EV management
- RFID card: £10 optional

**What they do well:**
- Community trust — 12 years of data
- Largest UK public charger database
- Strong SEO — ranks for almost every "EV charger near me" query
- Proven monetisation: EV drivers pay £3–4/month for convenience

**Where they fall short:**
- Zero private/P2P charging capability — no homeowner listings
- No booking or reservation — first-come first-served only
- No OCPP smart charger integration
- No voice, no AI, no automation
- No host earnings — can't earn from your charger
- No marketplace
- Revenue is thin: subscriptions + Zap-Pay processing fees
- Owned by Autotravel Group — not an EV-native company; slow to innovate

**Zipgrid vs Zap-Map:** Zap-Map is a map. Zipgrid is a platform. We integrate Zap-Map's public charger data via OCPI in Phase 3, making Zipgrid a *superset* — every charger Zap-Map shows, plus 10,000+ private Zipgrid listings Zap-Map never will.

### 2.2 Pod Point (EDF Energy)

**What they are:** UK's largest home and workplace charger installer. 225,000+ charge points. Acquired by EDF in 2020.

**Pricing:**
- PAYG at public sites (Tesco, Lidl, VW Group)
- Home charger subscription: £34–40/month over 36 months (includes hardware + installation — own the charger after term)
- Pod Rewards: cashback on charging

**What they do well:**
- Largest installed base of home chargers in the UK
- Trusted brand — OZEV-approved, quality hardware
- Smart scheduling on own hardware
- Partnership distribution (Tesco, Lidl)

**Where they fall short:**
- **Hardware lock-in:** smart features only work with Pod Point charger
- **No P2P:** cannot share your charger or earn from it
- **No voice, no AI, no agentic automation**
- **No marketplace** for installers or accessories
- App is functional but dated — Pod Point's incentive is to sell chargers, not innovate the app
- EDF ownership means energy company priorities, not tech company speed

**Zipgrid vs Pod Point:** Pod Point owns the installation channel. Zipgrid builds on top of it — we integrate Pod Point chargers via OCPP and add the P2P, voice, and AI layer they will never build because it cannibalises their hardware revenue.

- Fleet tools are basic, aimed at enterprise not SMB

**Zipgrid vs BP Pulse:** No real overlap. BP Pulse owns motorway charging. Zipgrid owns driveways and business car parks. In Phase 3, OCPI integration lets Zipgrid drivers use BP Pulse chargers from the Zipgrid app and pay via Zipgrid Wallet — making Zipgrid the single app for all charging.

## 3. Public Charger Strategy — Yes, Zipgrid Supports Them

**The question was: does Zipgrid support public chargers?**

**Answer: Yes — fully, via a two-phase integration strategy.**

### Phase 1 (Launch): OCPP-only
At launch, Zipgrid shows only OCPP-paired chargers (private hosts). Drivers use the map for private P2P bookings. Public charger overlay is not shown — we focus on building supply density.

### Phase 3: OCPI Integration — All UK public chargers on the map
OCPI 2.2.1 protocol connects Zipgrid to the public charging roaming network. This gives us:
- All 60,000+ UK public charge points on the Zipgrid map
- Ability to start/stop and pay for public sessions from the Zipgrid app
- Zipgrid Wallet used to pay at public chargers (earn Rewards points too)
- Single app for all charging — private and public

**Why Phase 3 and not Phase 1:**
Building public charger integration before supply-side density would expose us as a worse version of Zap-Map. We establish ourselves in the private charging niche first, then expand to subsume the public charging category — not compete on it from day one.

### Revenue on public charging:
- Zipgrid processes payment via Wallet for public charging → earn a small transaction fee (1–2%)
- Driver earns Rewards points on public charging sessions
- Driver Plus subscription includes public charging access as a benefit

## 6. Why Investors Are Interested in This Space

You mentioned seeing investors back map-only apps. Here's why:

- **Zap-Map raised a Series B** — market validation that EV charging apps are fundable
- **Network effect moats:** every new host listing makes the platform more valuable to drivers, and vice versa — classic marketplace compounding
- **Recurring revenue:** subscriptions + transaction fees = predictable ARR that investors value at 8–15× revenue
- **Infrastructure play:** owning the charging network layer (not the hardware) is an asset-light, high-margin business model
- **Exit liquidity:** bp, Shell, EDF, Octopus, and every major charger OEM would pay a premium to acquire a platform that owns 100,000+ host relationships and 1M+ driver accounts

If a map app got funded, a voice + AI + P2P + marketplace platform at better economics gets funded at a higher valuation. The pitch is straightforward.

## 7. Extended Competitor Landscape — Discovery, Routing & Networks

*Added in Planning Baseline v2.2 — September 30, 2026*

### 7.1 PlugShare (Recurrent Auto)

**What they are:** The world's largest community-driven EV charging map. 650,000+ charge points globally, 3M+ users. Acquired by Recurrent Auto (battery intelligence startup) in 2022.

**Pricing:**
- Free: map, community check-ins, filters
- PlugShare Premium ($4.99/month): route planner, saved routes, ad-free

**What they do well:**
- Enormous global database — strongest in North America
- Community check-ins are genuinely useful real-time availability signals
- Hardware-agnostic

**Where they fall short:**
- No booking — cannot reserve a charger
- No P2P / private host listings
- No AI, no voice, no automation
- No earnings for charger owners
- Community-maintained = accuracy degrades over time
- No payments processing — directs to each network's own payment

**Zipgrid vs PlugShare:** PlugShare is Wikipedia. Zipgrid is Airbnb. PlugShare crowdsources information. Zipgrid enables transactions. The mental model shift from "find a charger" to "book, pay, and earn" is the core difference.

---

### 7.2 A Better Routeplanner (ABRP)

**What they are:** The dominant EV trip planning tool. Used by serious EV drivers to plan long-distance journeys with accurate battery consumption modelling. 1M+ active users.

**Pricing:**
- Free: basic route planning
- Premium ($2.99/month): live data, more vehicles, better accuracy

**What they do well:**
- Unrivalled battery consumption modelling — accounts for weather, elevation, speed, HVAC
- Integrates with Tronity, OVMS, Teslamate for live SoC
- CarPlay and Android Auto integration
- Open API — charger networks can integrate their availability data

**Where they fall short:**
- Pure trip planning — no booking, no payment, no P2P
- No AI assistant — excellent at computing routes, not conversational
- No homeowner/host features whatsoever
- Passive tool — user does the thinking; ABRP does the maths

**Zipgrid vs ABRP:** ABRP is a calculator. Zipgrid's AI is a travel companion. Zipgrid's trip planner (`/trip/plan`) ingests the same consumption logic but wraps it in an AI agent that books, confirms, and adapts in real time. Phase 3: partnership opportunity — ABRP handoffs to Zipgrid booking where ABRP's route hits a Zipgrid-listed charger.

---

### 7.3 IONITY

**What they are:** A joint venture by BMW, Ford, Hyundai, Mercedes, and VW Group operating ultra-rapid (150–350 kW) charging hubs across European motorways. 600+ locations, 3,000+ charge points.

**Pricing:**
- PAYG: £0.69–£0.79/kWh (premium for premium speed)
- IONITY Passport: varies by manufacturer — included with some BMW/VW premium subscriptions

**What they do well:**
- Fastest publicly available charging in Europe (350 kW)
- Premium motorway locations — purpose-built charging plazas
- Managed, reliable infrastructure (not community-reported)

**Where they fall short:**
- Entirely motorway/destination — no urban, residential, or workplace
- Premium pricing; not cost-effective for routine daily charging
- OEM-aligned — better value for BMW/Mercedes drivers on subscription
- Zero AI, voice, or intelligence

**Zipgrid vs IONITY:** No meaningful competition. IONITY handles motorway rapid charging. Zipgrid handles daily urban and residential charging. OCPI integration in Phase 3 adds IONITY chargers to the Zipgrid map — Zipgrid wallet can pay for IONITY stops on road trips.

---

### 7.4 Instavolt

**What they are:** The UK's largest owner-operated rapid charging network with a "no membership, no app, contactless only" philosophy. 700+ rapid chargers (50–150 kW) in retail parks and motorway services.

**Pricing:**
- PAYG contactless: ~79p/kWh
- No subscription, no membership, no app required by design

**What they do well:**
- Simplest possible charging experience — tap card, charge, go
- High uptime — regularly cited as the most reliable UK rapid network
- Growing rapidly in retail parks (Sainsbury's, ASDA partnerships)

**Where they fall short:**
- No smart features — no scheduling, no monitoring, no earnings
- PAYG-only; expensive for regular users
- "No app" philosophy means zero data, zero intelligence, zero loyalty
- No homeowner or SMB product

**Zipgrid vs Instavolt:** Instavolt is intentionally app-free. That's their positioning. Zipgrid adds them via OCPI in Phase 3 — Zipgrid wallet pays for Instavolt sessions, earning Rewards points Instavolt will never offer.

---

### 7.5 WattsUp / Fleet-Focused Startups

**What they are:** A category of B2B-focused fleet charging platforms (WattsUp, Mina, Monta, Fuuse, Bonnet) targeting company vehicle managers. Typical offering: mileage reimbursement, fleet usage reporting, depot charging management.

**Pricing:**
- Typically SaaS: £5–£20 per vehicle/month

**What they do well:**
- Automated home charging reimbursement (HMRC rate compliance)
- Fleet usage dashboards
- Integration with HR/expense systems

**Where they fall short:**
- B2B only — no consumer product, no homeowner tool
- No AI, no voice
- No P2P marketplace
- Focused on cost management, not revenue generation

**Zipgrid vs Fleet Startups:** Zipgrid's fleet module (Phase 2, migration 013) addresses exactly this market — but adds P2P network access, AI optimisation, and ESG reporting that none of these platforms offer. Zipgrid fleet = fleet management + marketplace + AI. The spend policy and reimbursement models already exist in the schema (`fleet_accounts`, `fleet_members`).

## 9. The Category Nobody Has Built

Every competitor solves one problem:
- **Zap-Map / PlugShare:** Where are the chargers?
- **BP Pulse / IONITY / Instavolt:** Here is a charger. Pay for it.
- **Pod Point / Ohme:** Here is hardware for your home.
- **ABRP:** Here is a route that accounts for your battery.
- **Fleet SaaS (Mina, Fuuse):** Here is your fleet charging report.

**Zipgrid is the only platform that answers:**
> "I need to charge. Handle everything."

That is not a feature. That is a category. And no one owns it.
