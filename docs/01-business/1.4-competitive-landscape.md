## 2. Competitor Deep Dives (Updated)

### 2.1 Zap-Map

**What they are:** The UK's dominant public charger discovery and community app. 500,000+ users, 30,000+ mapped charge points, community-reported availability.

**Pricing:**
- Free: map, live data, Zap-Pay, max 3 saved routes, ads
- Premium £29.99/yr (£2.50/mo) or £3.49/mo: unlimited routes, enhanced filters, CarPlay/Android Auto, ad-free, 5% Zap-Pay discount, EV management
- RFID card: £10 optional

**What they do well:**
- Community trust — 12 years of data
- Largest UK public charger database
- Strong SEO — ranks for almost every "EV charger near me" query
- Proven monetisation: EV drivers pay £3–4/month for convenience

**Where they fall short:**
- Zero private/P2P charging capability — no homeowner listings
- No booking or reservation — first-come first-served only
- No OCPP smart charger integration
- No voice, no AI, no automation
- No host earnings — can't earn from your charger
- No marketplace
- Revenue is thin: subscriptions + Zap-Pay processing fees
- Owned by Autotravel Group — not an EV-native company; slow to innovate

**Zipgrid vs Zap-Map:** Zap-Map is a map. Zipgrid is a platform. We integrate Zap-Map's public charger data via OCPI in Phase 3, making Zipgrid a *superset* — every charger Zap-Map shows, plus 10,000+ private Zipgrid listings Zap-Map never will.

### 2.2 Pod Point (EDF Energy)

**What they are:** UK's largest home and workplace charger installer. 225,000+ charge points. Acquired by EDF in 2020.

**Pricing:**
- PAYG at public sites (Tesco, Lidl, VW Group)
- Home charger subscription: £34–40/month over 36 months (includes hardware + installation — own the charger after term)
- Pod Rewards: cashback on charging

**What they do well:**
- Largest installed base of home chargers in the UK
- Trusted brand — OZEV-approved, quality hardware
- Smart scheduling on own hardware
- Partnership distribution (Tesco, Lidl)

**Where they fall short:**
- **Hardware lock-in:** smart features only work with Pod Point charger
- **No P2P:** cannot share your charger or earn from it
- **No voice, no AI, no agentic automation**
- **No marketplace** for installers or accessories
- App is functional but dated — Pod Point's incentive is to sell chargers, not innovate the app
- EDF ownership means energy company priorities, not tech company speed

**Zipgrid vs Pod Point:** Pod Point owns the installation channel. Zipgrid builds on top of it — we integrate Pod Point chargers via OCPP and add the P2P, voice, and AI layer they will never build because it cannibalises their hardware revenue.

### 2.3 BP Pulse

**What they are:** bp's public EV charging network. 9,000+ charge points across motorway services, retail parks, and on-street. Acquired Chargemaster in 2018.

**Pricing:**
- PAYG: ~79p/kWh (public rate)
- BP Pulse Membership: £7.85/month — up to 10% off charging, cheaper rapid/ultra-rapid rates, free RFID card

**What they do well:**
- National motorway coverage — critical for road trips
- Brand recognition (bp)
- Rapid and ultra-rapid chargers (50kW–150kW)
- Fleet solutions for large companies

**Where they fall short:**
- Entirely public network — no home or SMB integration
- Membership saves money but doesn't add features
- App is a payment terminal with a map — no intelligence
- No voice, no AI, no booking, no reviews
- Fleet tools are basic, aimed at enterprise not SMB

**Zipgrid vs BP Pulse:** No real overlap. BP Pulse owns motorway charging. Zipgrid owns driveways and business car parks. In Phase 3, OCPI integration lets Zipgrid drivers use BP Pulse chargers from the Zipgrid app and pay via Zipgrid Wallet — making Zipgrid the single app for all charging.

### 2.4 Shell Recharge

**What they are:** Shell's EV charging network via ubitricity acquisition. 5,000+ on-street lamppost chargers. Global energy brand.

**Pricing:**
- Free app, pay-as-you-go
- Shell Recharge Subscription: £9.99/month — 30% off app pricing (up to 1,000 kWh/month), Night Saver rates as low as 42p/kWh

**What they do well:**
- On-street lamppost chargers — unique infrastructure, no planning permission
- Strong council relationships
- Night Saver tariff is genuinely cheap (42p/kWh overnight)

**Where they fall short:**
- Slow Level 2 lamppost chargers only (3.7kW–7kW) — not suitable for rapid top-ups
- No home integration, no SMB, no marketplace
- App is minimal — tap to charge, that's it
- No innovation capability — Shell is an energy company moving slowly into tech

**Zipgrid vs Shell Recharge:** Shell Recharge's lamppost network complements Zipgrid — overnight street parking with slow charging. OCPI integration in Phase 3 adds Shell chargers to the Zipgrid map. No meaningful competition.

## 3. Public Charger Strategy — Yes, Zipgrid Supports Them

**The question was: does Zipgrid support public chargers?**

**Answer: Yes — fully, via a two-phase integration strategy.**

### Phase 1 (Launch): OCPP-only
At launch, Zipgrid shows only OCPP-paired chargers (private hosts). Drivers use the map for private P2P bookings. Public charger overlay is not shown — we focus on building supply density.

### Phase 3: OCPI Integration — All UK public chargers on the map
OCPI 2.2.1 protocol connects Zipgrid to the public charging roaming network. This gives us:
- All 60,000+ UK public charge points on the Zipgrid map
- Ability to start/stop and pay for public sessions from the Zipgrid app
- Zipgrid Wallet used to pay at public chargers (earn Rewards points too)
- Single app for all charging — private and public

**Why Phase 3 and not Phase 1:**
Building public charger integration before supply-side density would expose us as a worse version of Zap-Map. We establish ourselves in the private charging niche first, then expand to subsume the public charging category — not compete on it from day one.

### Revenue on public charging:
- Zipgrid processes payment via Wallet for public charging → earn a small transaction fee (1–2%)
- Driver earns Rewards points on public charging sessions
- Driver Plus subscription includes public charging access as a benefit

## 6. Why Investors Are Interested in This Space

You mentioned seeing investors back map-only apps. Here's why:

- **Zap-Map raised a Series B** — market validation that EV charging apps are fundable
- **Network effect moats:** every new host listing makes the platform more valuable to drivers, and vice versa — classic marketplace compounding
- **Recurring revenue:** subscriptions + transaction fees = predictable ARR that investors value at 8–15× revenue
- **Infrastructure play:** owning the charging network layer (not the hardware) is an asset-light, high-margin business model
- **Exit liquidity:** bp, Shell, EDF, Octopus, and every major charger OEM would pay a premium to acquire a platform that owns 100,000+ host relationships and 1M+ driver accounts

If a map app got funded, a voice + AI + P2P + marketplace platform at better economics gets funded at a higher valuation. The pitch is straightforward.
