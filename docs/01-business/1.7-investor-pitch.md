## Full Script — Slide by Slide

---

### SLIDE 1 — The Mandate

**What to show:** World map with 2030/2035 ban dates per country. Simple, bold typography. No clutter.

**Script:**
> "In 2035, the United Kingdom bans the sale of new petrol and diesel cars. So does the European Union. California, New York, and seventeen other US states. China's targets are even more aggressive.
>
> This is not a trend. This is government policy. It is legally mandated. Every new car sold from 2030 onward in these markets will be electric.
>
> One million EVs on UK roads today. Twenty million by 2035. Every single one of them needs somewhere to charge. Most of them will charge at home or at work.
>
> That is the market we are building for. And no one has built the right platform yet."

---

### SLIDE 2 — The Real Problem

**What to show:** Two side-by-side columns. Left: Driver pain. Right: Host pain. Clean icons, no cluttered lists.

**Script:**
> "Here is the reality today.
>
> Sarah is a teacher in South London. She has a £1,200 EV charger installed in her driveway. It sits idle for twenty hours every day while she is at work. She earns nothing from it. She manages it through three different apps depending on what she wants to do. And when it broke last month, she waited three days for a callback.
>
> Marcus drives an electric car in Hackney. He has no driveway. The nearest public rapid charger costs him seventy-nine pence per kilowatt-hour — that is one hundred and eighty pounds a month. It is always occupied. He cannot reserve it. He cannot plan around it.
>
> These are not edge cases. They are the majority of EV owners in the UK today.
>
> And every app in this space — Zap-Map, Pod Point, BP Pulse, Shell Recharge — was built for the public charging network. None of them solve what Sarah and Marcus actually need."

---

### SLIDE 3 — The Solution

**What to show:** Clean platform diagram showing the 5 layers — Charging, Journey, Business, Energy, Mobility. Minimal. No tech jargon.

**Script:**
> "We built Zipgrid.
>
> Zipgrid is the EV charging ecosystem platform — the operating system for home and business charging.
>
> Here is what makes it different from everything else in the market.
>
> First: it is a two-sided marketplace. Sarah lists her driveway. Marcus books it. She earns one hundred and forty pounds this month without opening the app once. Marcus pays thirty-two pence per kilowatt-hour instead of seventy-nine.
>
> Second: it is voice-first. Marcus says 'Book my nearest charger for tomorrow at ten' — and it is done. Sarah says 'Charge to eighty percent before seven am' — and it happens automatically, at the cheapest overnight rate.
>
> Third: it is agentic. When Sarah's charger threw a fault last week, Zipgrid detected it, diagnosed it as a loose connector, and booked a local certified engineer for the next day. Sarah did not lift a finger.
>
> No one else does any of this. Not Zap-Map. Not Pod Point. Not BP Pulse. Not a single one."

---

### SLIDE 4 — Product Demo Narrative

**What to show:** Three phone mockups side by side — driver map view, host earnings dashboard, voice overlay. Premium, dark mode shown.

**Script:**
> "Let me show you what this looks like in practice.
>
> A driver opens Zipgrid. They see a map. Not just public chargers — private driveways, business car parks, covered parking lots. Three listings within half a mile of their flat. All cheaper than any public rapid charger. All reservable. One tap — confirmed.
>
> A homeowner opens Zipgrid. They see this week's earnings. Ninety-four pounds. Next payout Monday. Their charger paired via OCPP so every session is tracked to the exact watt-hour. The AI is already suggesting they raise their price by four pence per kilowatt-hour based on nearby demand.
>
> And the voice layer runs underneath all of it. No screen needed for the most common actions. That is a genuine first in this market."

### SLIDE 5 — Market Size

**Script:**
> "The UK market alone is our first target.
>
> One-point-one million EVs on UK roads today. Seven hundred thousand home chargers installed. Fifty thousand small businesses with EV charging on site. Growing at twenty percent per year.
>
> Our total addressable market in the UK is one hundred and sixty million to two hundred and eighty million pounds per year across subscriptions, transaction fees, marketplace, and data.
>
> Our serviceable market — the portion we can reach with our product in the next three years — is seventy-five to one hundred and thirty million.
>
> And here is the key point: this market is not growing because of consumer preference alone. It is growing because governments in the UK, EU, and US have made it law. Policy is the tailwind. We are not fighting the tide. We are riding it."

### SLIDE 6 — Business Model

**What to show:** Simple revenue stream table. No dense text. Just the streams, rates, and phase.

**Script:**
> "Eleven revenue streams. Let me focus on the three that matter most.
>
> First: transaction commission. Every time a driver charges at a private listing, Zipgrid takes ten to fifteen percent. Average session is six to eight pounds. We take eighty pence to a pound. At twenty thousand sessions a month, that is sixteen thousand pounds a month from one stream alone.
>
> Second: subscriptions. Homeowners pay seven ninety-nine a month for smart scheduling and voice control. Small businesses pay twenty-five to one hundred a month. These are recurring, predictable, and sticky — hosts earning two hundred pounds a month from their charger do not cancel their eight-pound subscription.
>
> Third: marketplace commissions. Twelve percent on every installer job booked. Seven percent on hardware sales. This activates when we launch the marketplace in month nine.
>
> We reach breakeven at month sixteen. Year three revenue is seven point four million pounds. At a ten-times multiple, exit valuation is seventy-four to one hundred and thirty-three million pounds."

## Q&A Preparation

### Likely Investor Questions and Answers

**Q: What stops Zap-Map from building this?**
> "Zap-Map is owned by Autotravel Group — not a tech company. Their moat is their community data. Adding P2P marketplace, OCPP integration, insurance underwriting, and voice AI is a complete product rebuild. It would take them two to three years and cannibalise their existing revenue model. We have a structural head start."

**Q: What if Octopus Energy builds this?**
> "We approach Octopus as a distribution partner, not a competitor. Their value is in energy tariffs. Our value is in the marketplace and software layer. The fastest path for them is partnership — co-marketing to their six hundred thousand EV customers. We want to be their charging platform, not their competitor."

**Q: How do you handle the insurance?**
> "The platform umbrella policy is a Commercial General Liability policy placed by Zipgrid as policyholder. We are the insured party providing the benefit to hosts — this is the same model Airbnb uses with AirCover. It does not require FCA insurance distribution authorisation. We have engaged specialist brokers (Howden) and identified five underwriters."

**Q: What's the risk if EV adoption slows?**
> "The risk is low for two reasons. First, government policy mandates the transition — it is not optional. Second, we have eleven revenue streams. Even at fifty percent of projected EV growth, our subscription revenue from existing homeowners and SMBs sustains the business."

**Q: How defensible is the position once you have scale?**
> "Three moats. First: network effects — more hosts makes the platform more valuable to drivers and vice versa, compounding over time. Second: OCPP session data — at ten thousand paired chargers, we have the largest private EV charging dataset in the UK. No competitor can buy this. Third: host earnings lock-in — a homeowner earning two hundred pounds a month from Zipgrid does not switch platforms."
