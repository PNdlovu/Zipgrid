## 1. The Core Argument

Most technology markets grow because of consumer preference. They can stall, reverse, or plateau.

Zipgrid's market grows because of **law**.

Governments across the UK, EU, US, and Asia have committed to banning the sale of new petrol and diesel vehicles. This is not a forecast — it is legislation. The EV transition is not optional; it is mandated.

This fundamentally de-risks the market growth assumption. Every year that passes, more EVs require charging infrastructure. Every year that passes, more homeowners and businesses become potential Zipgrid hosts. The total addressable market expands automatically — not because we work harder, but because government policy drives it.

> **This is the first time in living memory that a technology market has been mandated by law across multiple major economies simultaneously.**

## 2. Global ICE Ban Timeline

### United Kingdom

| Policy | Date | Detail |
|--------|------|--------|
| Zero Emission Vehicles (ZEV) Mandate | 2024 onwards | 22% of all new car sales must be EV in 2024, rising to 80% by 2030 |
| New petrol/diesel car ban | **2035** | All new cars and vans must be zero-emission |
| All vehicles zero-emission | **2040** | Target for all road vehicles |
| ULEZ expansion | Ongoing | Ultra Low Emission Zone expanded to all London boroughs; penalises ICE vehicles |
| EVHS grant | Active | Electric Vehicle Homecharge Scheme — up to £350 toward home charger installation |
| Workplace Charging Scheme | Active | Government grant for workplace EV chargers |
| Building Regulations Part S | 2022 | New residential builds >10 parking spaces must include EV charge points |

**Impact on Zipgrid:** Every new home built in England must have an EV charge point. Every homeowner who uses the EVHS grant installs a charger that sits idle — and is a potential Zipgrid host.

## 4. Why Policy = Guaranteed Demand (The Investor Argument)

### Consumer preference markets vs. mandate markets

| Consumer Preference Market | Policy Mandate Market |
|---------------------------|----------------------|
| Can plateau or reverse | Cannot reverse (it is law) |
| Dependent on marketing | Dependent on legislation |
| Sensitive to economic downturns | Partially insulated (buying new car anyway) |
| Requires ongoing demand stimulation | Self-stimulating (every new car = new EV) |
| Example: social media apps | Example: EV charging infrastructure |

### The compound effect

```
Year 2026: 1.1M EVs on UK roads
Year 2028: ~1.7M EVs (estimated)
Year 2030: ~3.0M EVs (ZEV mandate enforcement)
Year 2032: ~5.5M EVs
Year 2035: ~10M+ EVs (ICE ban kicks in fully)

Each EV owner is a:
  • Potential Zipgrid driver (demand)
  • Potential Zipgrid host if they have a home charger (supply)
  • Potential Zipgrid fleet customer if they manage company EVs

The platform's total addressable market doubles roughly every 3 years
through 2035 — driven entirely by government policy, not marketing.
```

## 5. Policy Risk — What Could Go Wrong

Honest assessment for investors:

| Policy Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|-------------|------------|--------|-----------|
| UK delays or weakens 2035 ban | Low — politically committed | Medium — slows growth curve | Revenue model diversified; not solely dependent on new EV buyers |
| ZEV Mandate targets reduced | Low — recently legislated | Medium | Existing 1.1M EVs already create large addressable market |
| EVHS grant scheme ends | Medium — budget-dependent | Low | Reduces supply pipeline but doesn't remove existing installed base |
| EU adequacy decision lapses | Low | Low for UK launch | Data architecture supports SCCs as fallback |
| New government reverses EV policy | Very Low — cross-party consensus | High | Hedge: EU and US markets provide alternative if UK slows |

**Key point for investors:** Even if every government grant and mandate disappeared tomorrow, there are already 1.1 million EVs on UK roads — all needing to charge, most doing so at home. The installed base alone justifies the market.
