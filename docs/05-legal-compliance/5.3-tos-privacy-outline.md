### Section 4 — Booking, Sessions & Payments (Drivers)

**Intent to convey:**

**Bookings:**
- A confirmed booking is a contract between the driver and the host. Zipgrid facilitates the contract but is not a party to it.
- By confirming a booking, the driver authorises Zipgrid to place a hold on their payment method for the estimated session cost plus a reasonable buffer for idle fees.
- The actual charge is calculated based on energy delivered (kWh × rate) plus any applicable idle fees, and captured at session end.

**Session conduct:**
- Drivers must arrive within their booked window. Late arrivals do not extend the booking end time.
- Drivers must not access the host's property outside the booked time window, even if they still have a valid access code.
- Drivers must treat host property with reasonable care. Damage caused by a driver may be claimed against the driver by the host, facilitated by Zipgrid's incident process.
- Idle fees apply automatically when a vehicle remains connected for more than 15 minutes after charging is complete. Drivers pre-authorise idle fees at booking.

**Payments:**
- Payment is processed by Stripe. Zipgrid never stores card details.
- The total charge includes: energy cost + idle fees (if any) + applicable peak surcharge + VAT.
- Zipgrid's platform fee (currently 10–15%) is deducted from the host's payout — it is NOT an additional charge to the driver beyond the stated session price.
- Receipts are issued by email and available in-app within 24 hours of session completion.

**Cancellations & Refunds:**
- Full refund: if cancelled by driver more than 24 hours before scheduled start.
- 50% refund: if cancelled by driver between 2–24 hours before scheduled start.
- No refund: if cancelled less than 2 hours before start or for no-shows (with evidence from host or OCPP data).
- Full refund always provided: if host cancels, charger is faulty on arrival, or session cannot be started due to charger fault.
- Refunds processed within 5–10 business days to the original payment method.

## PART C — LEGAL DRAFTING INSTRUCTIONS

The following notes are for the solicitor who will draft the final documents:

### Priority Issues to Address

1. **Zipgrid's classification as a marketplace vs. service provider** — Ensure ToS clearly and defensibly positions Zipgrid as an intermediary marketplace (like Airbnb) not a service provider (like a utility). This is critical for limiting liability for charger faults and driver/host behaviour.

2. **Host Protection Guarantee structure** — The guarantee must be described accurately in the ToS so it is not inadvertently characterised as an insurance product being distributed to hosts (which would require FCA authorisation). It should be described as a platform benefit — a CGL policy held by Zipgrid for the benefit of users.

3. **Payment terms and consumer cooling-off rights** — Under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, there is a 14-day right to cancel digital services unless performance has begun with the consumer's consent. The booking/session model requires careful drafting to establish that once a session begins, the cooling-off right is waived.

4. **GDPR consent for voice features** — Voice transcripts require explicit consent as a distinct lawful basis. The consent mechanism, what users are consenting to, and the withdrawal mechanism must be clearly drafted and implemented technically.

5. **Installer independent contractor agreement** — A separate, more detailed contractor agreement is needed for the marketplace installer relationships. The ToS Section 8 is a summary only.

6. **Jurisdiction clause** — England and Wales jurisdiction clause must be qualified for consumer users: it cannot deprive them of mandatory consumer protections in their home jurisdiction (relevant for future EU expansion).

7. **Arbitration vs. court** — Consider whether a mandatory mediation step (before court) is appropriate for the dispute resolution section. Must not exclude consumers' right to use courts.
