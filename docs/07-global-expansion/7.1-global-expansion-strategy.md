## 1. Expansion Philosophy

> "Get to market. Win market share. Prove the model. Then grow."

Zipgrid does not expand until the previous market is profitable and defensible. The UK is not a test market — it is the first market to dominate. Everything else is built on that foundation.

**Expansion readiness criteria:**
1. Current market: 10,000+ active listings and 100,000+ registered users
2. Unit economics: positive contribution margin per transaction
3. Team: dedicated country manager and support capacity in place
4. Legal: local legal counsel engaged and compliance confirmed
5. Insurance: local/EU CGL policy coverage confirmed
6. Technical: i18n, local currency, local payment methods integrated

## 2. Data Residency — Confirmed Architecture

### UK Residents → EU (Amsterdam) Storage

**Legal basis:** UK GDPR adequacy decision for EEA. UK→EU data flows are lawful without SCCs.

**Infrastructure:**
- Railway `eu-west` region = Amsterdam, Netherlands (AWS eu-west-1 underlying)
- All services, database, cache, and media stored in `eu-west`
- Amsterdam provides < 8ms latency to London — indistinguishable from UK hosting

**When EU expansion happens:**
- Same Amsterdam infrastructure serves all EU users — no data migration required
- EU users' data is already in the EU — GDPR Article 45 fully satisfied
- For US/Americas expansion: spin up `us-east` Railway region; implement data residency routing by user geography

### Data Residency Routing (Phase 4 — Americas)

```typescript
/**
 * @file data-residency-middleware.ts
 * @description Routes user data to appropriate regional database
 * based on user's country of residence.
 * UK + EU users → eu-west (Amsterdam)
 * US + Canada users → us-east (Virginia)
 * APAC users → ap-southeast (Singapore) [Phase 5]
 */

const REGION_DB_URLS: Record<DataRegion, string> = {
  'eu-west':      process.env.DATABASE_URL_EU_WEST!,
  'us-east':      process.env.DATABASE_URL_US_EAST!,
  'ap-southeast': process.env.DATABASE_URL_AP_SOUTHEAST!,
}

export function getRegionalDb(userCountry: string): DatabaseClient {
  const region = getRegionForCountry(userCountry)
  return createDbClient(REGION_DB_URLS[region])
}
```

### Phase 3: Europe (Months 30–48)

#### Wave 1: Ireland (Month 30)

**Why Ireland first:**
- English language — no localisation cost
- Similar regulatory framework to UK (Data Protection Act 2018, similar to UK GDPR)
- Octopus Energy already operates in Ireland (partnership leverage)
- SEAI (Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland) actively promotes EV infrastructure
- OZEV equivalent: SEAI EV grant scheme
- Currency: EUR (Stripe already configured for GBP+EUR)

**Preparation:**
- [ ] Irish Companies Registration Office: register Irish branch or subsidiary
- [ ] Irish Data Protection Commission: register as data controller
- [ ] Localise for Irish English (`en-IE` locale, EUR currency)
- [ ] Irish charger brands: EasyGo, Blink, ESB eCars — OCPP integration
- [ ] Irish insurance: CGL policy extending to Republic of Ireland
- [ ] Irish address regulations: Eircode geocoding

**Target 12 months post-launch:**
- 500 active listings (Dublin-first)
- 5,000 registered users
- £400k additional ARR

---

#### Wave 2: Netherlands (Month 36)

**Why Netherlands:**
- Highest EV penetration in Europe (40%+ of new car sales are EV)
- Amsterdam data centre — lowest latency to Dutch users (same datacenter!)
- Strong cycling + sustainability culture = EV-friendly population
- Charger brands: ENGIE, Allego, EV-Box, Alfen — all OCPP-compliant
- English widely spoken — reduces localisation urgency

**Preparation:**
- [ ] Dutch entity: Zipgrid BV (Besloten Vennootschap — Netherlands Ltd equivalent)
- [ ] Dutch DPA (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens): register as data controller
- [ ] `nl` locale: Dutch translation of all UI strings + KB articles
- [ ] Netherlands energy tariffs: Tibber API (dominant smart EV tariff)
- [ ] Type 2 dominant (same as UK) — no charger type changes needed
- [ ] VAT: Dutch 21% VAT (register for NL VAT)
- [ ] SEPA: bank transfer payment method for Dutch drivers (alongside cards)

**Target 12 months post-launch:**
- 800 active listings (Amsterdam + Rotterdam)
- 8,000 registered users
- £700k additional ARR

---

#### Wave 3: Germany (Month 42)

**Why Germany:**
- Largest EV market in EU by volume (1M+ EVs)
- Volkswagen Group / BMW / Mercedes domestic → high EV ownership
- Strong energy market reform (Energiewende) → smart charging alignment
- Complex regulatory environment (requires full German legal review)

**Key considerations:**
- `de` locale: German translation (professional translator — not AI-only)
- German data protection: BDSG compliance + GDPR (stricter enforcement)
- German charger brands: Keba, Wallbe, smartENERGY, EnBW
- Payment: SEPA + German bank transfer preference (lower card usage than UK)
- Energy tariff: Tibber Germany API, ENBW tariff API
- Legal: German eBusiness regulations, Fernabsatzgesetz (distance selling)

### Phase 4: Americas (Month 48+)

#### United States

**Market size:** 3M+ EVs (2026), growing 40% YoY. Massive TAM.

**Entry strategy:** Not direct launch — enter via partnership with US EV charging platform (similar to OCPI roaming model). Study market for 12 months before committing to full launch.

**Key differences from UK:**
- Plug standards: CCS1 + NACS (Tesla standard now open) vs. CCS2 + Type 2 in UK
- Electricity pricing: state-by-state regulation (California CPUC, Texas PUCT, etc.)
- Currency: USD (Stripe already configured)
- Legal: state-by-state compliance (California, Texas, Florida priority)
- Data privacy: CCPA (California) + growing state-level privacy laws
- Charger brands: ChargePoint, Blink, Wallbox US, JuiceBox

**Data residency for US:** Railway `us-east` (Virginia) — US user data must stay in US for CCPA compliance.

**Infrastructure:**
- New Railway region: `us-east`
- New Pinecone index: `us-east-1`
- US entity: Zipgrid Inc. (Delaware C-Corp — standard for US venture/investment)

**Target Y1 US:**
- 1,000 active listings (California first — San Francisco Bay Area, LA)
- 15,000 registered users
- $1.5M additional ARR

---
